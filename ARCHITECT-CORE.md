# ARCHITECT-CORE.md — the ONE architect law for every reel this org cuts

Read by all three channels: the daily IG routines (via ROUTINE-CORE.md),
interactive `/ig-reel` sessions (via the skill), and customer jobs (via
JOBS.md). The CHANNEL file defines who the reel is for, what may be read,
its guards, and its deliverables; THIS file defines how a concept is
architected and the craft every cut obeys. Where a channel file conflicts,
the channel file wins — it knows its own guards.

Why this file exists (2026-07-21): the bank-check law used to live as three
hand-maintained copies and drifted — the daily routine had it, the skill and
the customer pipeline didn't, and every customer reel came out of one
template. Craft lessons get fixed HERE, once, and every channel inherits
them. Channel-specific mechanics stay in the channel files.

## 1. Trend intelligence FIRST (mandatory, before any concept or cut decision)

Open the viral bank every run — this is the architect's first act, not a
late check:
- **`~/Claude Code/viral-bank/playbook.md`** — THE FORMAT LAYER: what's
  going viral RIGHT NOW, each entry with a `pairs-with`/splice idea. A
  format needs no banked clip — the doc exists so you can rebuild the
  format from the channel's own material.
- **`~/Claude Code/viral-bank/catalog.md`** — banked CLIPS, walked EVERY run
  by the DONOR PASS below (FRESH only unless you justify a STALE in writing).
- **`~/Claude Code/viral-bank/audio.md`** — trending-audio candidates.
- **`~/Claude Code/ig-pulse/state/shipped.md`** — THE SPIKE LAYER (added 2026-08-28, Marcus).
  What the spike radar caught and what was cut from it, newest first. Read it for two reasons: a
  spike is a format the feed is rewarding RIGHT NOW and may not have reached `playbook.md` yet, and
  the row tells you whether this org has already mimicked it and how that went. **The captures
  themselves now flow into `catalog.md` / `playbook.md` via PULSE.md step 8, so from 2026-08-28 the
  bank is the single surface — but the three captures made BEFORE that date are unbanked and this
  file is the only index of them.** A missing or empty file is a log note, never a failure.
- **`~/Claude Code/viral-bank/lessons.md`** — THE MECHANISMS LAYER: creators
  teaching HOW virality works. TRAINING ONLY — read it to internalize the
  levers (hook retention, loop/rewatch, pattern interrupt), then apply them to
  the channel's OWN material. NEVER a splice source; its clips are off-limits
  to any cut.

**FORMAT-FIRST IS THE DEFAULT (Marcus, 2026-08-21 — "the viral bank always
should be the first").** Architect from the current meta: which 🔥 LIVE format
maps onto the material this channel actually has? **The reel is BUILT ON one.**
An original concept is the EXCEPTION, not a co-equal option — to ship one, the
notes must name **every** live entry that fits today's footage and say why the
original beats each, and QA treats an unjustified pass as **FIX-FIRST**. If
nothing live fits the material, that is a stop-and-say to the channel owner,
not a licence to invent. A missing/empty bank is a log note and you proceed;
never a failure. Never scrape a clip or research a format ad hoc mid-run — if
it's not in the bank, it's not available today.

*The cost of the old wording, which made "consciously PASSED" a legal outcome:*
@imabgold shipped nine consecutive aired reels with no format at all
(2026-08-10 to 08-20). Comments and shares went to zero across three straight
posts and reach collapsed from ~1,700 to ~200. The two reels immediately before
that streak, both built on playbook #26, still hold every engagement record the
account has (40 likes, 11 comments, 16 shares, 8 saves).

**REACH mode (product invariant, all channels):** optimize for the most
viral reel; the channel's own material must stay essential/load-bearing
(never disposable filler), but a banked clip or borrowed format MAY carry
the payoff beat. A splice is declared in the notes/outline with the
provenance QA needs to verify it.

**THE DONOR PASS IS MANDATORY, EVERY RUN (Marcus, 2026-08-22).** Once the
format is chosen, walk the `FRESH` entries in `catalog.md` against the reel's
beats, using each entry's `pairs-with` line as the match test (narrow by the
entry's lane tag first — that is what the tag is for — then read every
FRESH entry in the matching lanes, and the whole FRESH set when no lane
matches). The notes must carry a **DONOR PASS** record: the top 3 candidates
BY SHORTCODE, each with its `pairs-with` line quoted and the reel beat it was
matched to, and for each one not used, ONE of these four rejection names:
- `LITERAL-REFERENT` — §6.1: the clip is not a picture of the sentence.
- `HOOK-LEGIBILITY` — §6.1: the card word it would sit under reads as a
  different English word outside the niche.
- `DONOR-LEGIBILITY` — §6.2: the subject is unreadable at reel speed.
- `OWN-IS-REFERENT` — the §6.1 corollary: own footage IS the literal
  referent of that beat, so the donor adds nothing.
**THE DECISION MUST BE MADE, NOT PASSED (Marcus, 2026-08-27).** This is the
half the 2026-08-22 rule left open, and it is why the pass kept producing NONE
anyway. Three things are now required, and QA checks all three:

1. **WALK THE WHOLE FRESH SET.** Lane-first is a reading ORDER, never a
   stopping point. You do not stop at the first three you can reject, and you
   do not stop at the end of one lane. The three shortcodes in the record are
   the best three OF THE ENTIRE WALK, chosen after seeing all of it. (Job
   `8a0fc66a`, 2026-08-27: the record named 3 candidates out of 28 FRESH
   entries in the one lane it opened, and stopped. That is a pass wearing a
   record.)
2. **TEST EACH CANDIDATE BOTH WAYS BEFORE REJECTING IT.** Ask whether the clip
   reproduces the JOKE, and separately whether it lends an ELEMENT (see
   "Playbook fidelity" above and §6.1). A clip that fails as a whole-joke
   reproduction can still be the right 1-2s borrow, and rejecting it on the
   first test alone is not a considered rejection. Say in the record which
   test each candidate failed.
3. **`NONE` CARRIES A POSITIVE ARGUMENT, NOT JUST FOUR REJECTIONS.** Rejecting
   every candidate is the START of the answer. Finish it: name the single
   strongest donor the walk surfaced, and say why THIS reel is stronger
   without it. "Nothing matched" is not that sentence. If you cannot write one
   honest sentence for why the reel is better without its best available
   donor, you have not decided yet — you have declined to.

The burden of proof sits on NOT splicing. That is deliberately the opposite of
how a tired run behaves, because no is always the cheaper outcome to defend:
four rejection names exist and there is no paperwork for a yes.

**This is NOT a quota and NOT splice-by-default** — Marcus ruled that out on
2026-08-22 and it stands. A reel that is genuinely stronger whole is the right
answer and always has been. What is banned is reaching that answer without
having really looked.

`Splice: NONE` is a legal outcome ONLY on top of that record, and an honest
all-`OWN-IS-REFERENT` record is a legal NONE. A bare `Splice: NONE`, or a
`NONE` with no named shortcodes, or a corollary cited without candidates = the
catalog was not read = **FIX-FIRST at QA**, the same teeth as the §6.0
transient scan. **Thin catalog:** fewer than 3 FRESH entries → name every
FRESH entry there is and say so; an empty catalog is a log note, never a
FIX-FIRST (same grace as the format layer above). Where own footage cannot
literally be the payoff referent, a banked donor is the EXPECTED answer on
that beat, not the exception; the architect still decides, but it decides on
the record.

*Why:* the bank grew to ~50 FRESH clips and a 245-line audio pool while the
routines kept shipping `Splice: NONE`. 2026-08-22's @imabgold run skipped the
whole catalog on a one-line corollary and a one-day kill read. "Only if it
makes the reel more viral" defaults to no when nobody is made to look; the
record makes looking the cheap path.

**Playbook fidelity (borrowed formats):** when the concept reproduces a
viral-bank playbook format, reproduce its joke practically verbatim. The
formats are intricate and the wording is load-bearing — you swap the
FOOTAGE, never rewrite the JOKE. Edits are allowed only when they preserve
the exact mechanism; a "clever" or meta rewrite that concedes or inverts the
format's premise breaks it (2026-07-22: an imabgold reel took playbook #12,
the "made by a REAL human" flex, and wrote "an AI cut this one too" as the
middle beat — conceding the very thing the joke flexes against, which made
it nonsense). Put the verbatim playbook entry in the staged notes and hand
it to QA so the reproduction is checkable.

**REPRODUCTION vs ELEMENT — declare which one you are doing (lifted to the core
2026-08-27; it was @imabgold performance.md lesson 20 and no customer job or
routine could see it).** Verbatim fidelity applies when you reproduce the
JOKE. It does NOT apply when you borrow an ELEMENT — the specific thing a
catalog entry's `the-beat:` line explicitly offers to lend — and supply your
own setup around it. Both are legal borrows. **Declare which it is, and
fidelity is judged against that declaration.**

*Why this had to move.* The core said only "reproduce the joke verbatim," so
every architect outside @imabgold ran exactly one test on every donor: *is this
clip a picture of my sentence?* On a customer job the answer is structurally
always no (see the ordering trap below), so the bank was walked and emptied
every time. @imabgold spliced seven times in the same period, out of the same
bank, because lesson 20 lived in its file.

**THE ORDERING TRAP, and the fix (2026-08-27).** `OWN-IS-REFERENT` is
self-fulfilling whenever the cards are written by looking at the footage: you
cannot write a sentence your own footage fails to picture when the footage is
where the sentence came from. Job `8a0fc66a`'s record ended, in its own words,
"the bank holds no picture of a sunset outdoing a building, because that
picture is a sunset" — a tautology, not a finding. **So draft the beat list and
its cards from the CHOSEN FORMAT first, then assign footage to beats.** That
ordering is what makes `OWN-IS-REFERENT` a real test with a real failure mode,
and it is the ordering @imabgold already uses. A beat the uploads cannot cover
is not a problem to design around; §1 says it is exactly where a banked donor
is the expected answer.

**TRUST THE CLIP, NOT ITS CATALOG LINE.** `the-beat:` and `pairs-with:` are
notes taken at capture time and they can be wrong about their own footage.
Before designing a beat around a borrowed action, VERIFY THE ACTION IS THERE —
step the actual window and watch the motion, do not infer it from the
description. (2026-08-27: `DcejaQYskRm`'s line promises "the cat crosses the
frame and leaves, unimpressed." The cat never moves. A whole beat was designed,
built and killed at QA on a sentence the file made up.)

## 2. The weighing (how a concept is chosen)

Weigh three inputs; never let a single one dictate:
1. **The current meta** (§1) — the forward-looking input.
2. **The material + what's proven for THIS audience** — the channel's
   footage and its performance rules/lanes (channel file names them; a
   customer job has no history — the material itself is the second input).
3. **Any standing brief** from Marcus or the customer (the channel file
   defines the mechanism: next-concept.md, marcus_note, spoken narration).

Target: a currently-viral format landed on the channel's OWN material,
inside its niche. Rear-view "what works" rules are CONSTRAINTS you check
the finished concept against — not the thing you build from. A concept that
satisfies only the rear-view rules with the bank unread is an incomplete
architect step. The decision must be AUDITABLE: the notes/outline name the
LIVE format the reel is BUILT ON, or carry the full §1 pass justification.
**Input 1 leads.** Inputs 2 and 3 shape which live format is chosen and what
footage lands on it; they do not license skipping the format layer.

## 3. Craft canon (every cut, every channel)

- **Beats and cuts:** 2-3+ distinct beats minimum; cut on pose/motion
  changes (a cut inside one continuous motion reads as a stutter); never
  one static unbroken shot.
- **Visual variety:** no two CONSECUTIVE beats at the same framing and the
  same scale. Beats from one continuous scene get punch-ins/outs (10-30%
  zoom crops, alternating) so the cut has rhythm, not a slideshow feel. A
  deliberate static repeat for comedic effect must be declared.
- **Text:** house style — white Arial Bold
  (`/System/Library/Fonts/Supplemental/Arial Bold.ttf`),
  `borderw=3-4:bordercolor=black@0.7-0.8:shadowcolor=black@0.5:shadowx=2:shadowy=2`
  (heavier border on bright footage), centered, `textfile=` to avoid
  escaping. **SAFE ZONE (hard):** the block starts no higher than y=250 at
  1920 height (~13% down — IG crops above) and clears the bottom IG UI
  zone (the channel file sets its exact floor: routines/skill use ~25%,
  customer jobs 320px). **HORIZONTAL edge-crop law (Marcus, 2026-07-31,
  applies to every channel): IG also crops the side edges — a Randmar
  clip that hit 100k lost its card that way. No text line may exceed
  ~85% of frame width (~915px at 1080; keep ≥80px margins). A longer
  line BREAKS TO TWO LINES, each centered independently (two drawtext
  calls ~95px apart at fontsize 72 — a single multiline textfile
  left-aligns its lines inside the box, which reads wrong on centered
  cards).**
- **Hook law:** the on-screen hook is a broad, relatable feeling/joke an
  outsider parses on first read (metaphors must survive a LITERAL reading).
  Product/insider specifics live in a PS line + the caption, never in the
  hook.
- **Payoff LAST, loop-aware:** front-loading the reveal kills the rewatch;
  give text beats time to read; the last line should recontextualize the
  first so the loop rewards a second watch.
- **Truth gate:** claims must match the pixels they sit on, and every
  on-screen or caption claim is fact-checked before shipping.
  - **ORDINALS ARE CHECKED AGAINST THE QUEUE, NOT THE ARCHIVE** (2026-08-18,
    @imabgold `post_51`). A claim like "post 51", "day 9", "the third time"
    is falsified by anything ALREADY STAGED that posts first, not only by
    what has already published. The API said `media_count=50` and the claim
    was still wrong, because another finished reel was sitting in the
    delivery folder holding the same slot. Check what is queued, and leave
    exactly one reel staged per slot.
  - **A LIVE COUNTER BURNED INTO PIXELS IS A POST-TIME CHECK, NOT A BUILD-TIME
    ONE** (same run). Follower counts, post counts, streak days and prices
    move between staging and posting, and a hook that invites the viewer to
    verify a number gets verified by exactly the people it is aimed at. If a
    number is in the cut, the staged doc must OPEN with how to re-check it
    and how long a re-render takes.
- **Color law (Marcus, 2026-07-24): NEVER tonemap the footage. Deliver the
  camera's own lighting.** iPhone clips are HLG HDR 10-bit; the old habit of
  converting to SDR (`tonemap=hable`, npl=100) before cutting shipped washed-out
  reels — lifted shadows, blown highlights ("looks like a filter" — Marcus, after
  two deliveries). Cut, crop, scale and drawtext in native 10-bit and encode
  HEVC 10-bit with the source's color tags carried through
  (`-pix_fmt yuv420p10le -color_primaries bt2020 -color_trc arib-std-b67
  -colorspace bt2020nc -tag:v hvc1`). Tonemapped SDR conversion is for the
  AGENT'S OWN frame inspection only, never for a deliverable. Verify with
  `signalstats` YAVG: deliverable vs source should match to <1%.
- **Length:** short and loopable is the default (3-6s POV comedy; the
  channel file sets its exact windows). Longer only for a true multi-scene
  arc with a reason to exist.
- **SCREEN LAW — NO BLURS, EVER (Marcus, 2026-08-23; scope widened to EVERY
  channel 2026-08-26). This binds architect, builder AND QA on all three
  channels.** Never apply a blur, a pixelation, a scrub band, a black rect or
  any other obscuring treatment to a screen in the footage. Not silently, not
  as a "safe default", not as a stopgap while waiting for a ruling. If a
  screen is in frame, it ships as shot. It does not matter whether the screen
  was staged or incidental, and it does not matter whose footage it is —
  2026-08-23 settled his own accounts ("I don't want any blurs whatsoever.
  Leave the screens as they are... I want that rule removed"), and 2026-08-26
  settled customer jobs on reel job `0d8e7c1f`, where a stranger's laptop had
  a legible chat window in the hook beat: *"You shouldn't blur this screen."*
  - **The duty that replaces blurring is REPORTING.** Read every visible
    screen at full resolution. If it carries something the owner would
    plausibly want to know is going public — a credentials path, a token, a
    client name, someone else's data, unreleased numbers — say in one plain
    line exactly what a viewer can read, then follow the ruling. He has
    consistently chosen to ship.
  - **Concealment by framing counts as a blur** when concealment is its
    reason. Choosing a better composition or trimming a beat is always fair
    game; shifting a crop *specifically* to push something out of frame is
    the same act as blurring it, and gets reported instead.
  - **QA must NOT fail a reel for a readable screen and must NOT ask for a
    blur.** Brief every reviewer on this line explicitly — a reviewer handed
    the old rule will keep reporting readable screens as violations. The only
    screen content that is a genuine stop is the channel file's own content
    line (for customer jobs, JOBS.md guard 4: readable documents, IDs or
    financial papers). An app UI or a chat window is not that.
  - *Cost of not having this here:* it lived only in @imabgold's brand.md and
    the /ig-reel skill, so on 2026-08-26 a customer job's architect specced a
    feathered blur over a laptop, its independent QA passed it, and the
    delivered hook beat shipped a blue smear across a quarter of the frame.
    Both agents followed the written law correctly. The law was in the wrong
    file.

## 4. Audio law

- A pick is a TITLE and an ARTIST — never a description of how to find one,
  never an invented track, never a track you cannot source.
- Pick from `viral-bank/audio.md` first; state why it fits THIS cut and
  where to start it. When the cut carries a splice, say how the audio sits on
  the donor beat (the drop/transient lands on the cut into it, or the pick is
  silent there on purpose) — the pool is large enough that a default pick is
  a skipped decision.
- **Never bake licensed/trending audio into a deliverable.** Trending audio
  is added natively in-app at post time (IG's license covers in-app use
  only; baked copyrighted audio gets business accounts muted/throttled).
  The only exception is a channel's explicitly licensed local bed library
  (e.g. `reel-app/preview-beds/`) for preview-only renders, per the
  channel file.
- Where the channel keeps an audio ledger, respect its variety rule and
  append the pick when staging.

## 5. QA shape (every deliverable)

- QA is a FRESH, independent agent with zero context on how the piece was
  cut. No PASS, no delivery.
- The brief hands over: the output file(s), frame strips + stills, the
  concept, and the channel's gates — text legibility + safe zone, typos,
  cuts/continuity/loop, the channel's privacy rules, §3 canon compliance
  (variety rule included), and the channel's provenance gate where it has
  one (cut-today for routines, outline-match for jobs).
- QA verifies the DECISION RECORD too: the notes/outline carry the §2
  format line; its absence means the trend step was skipped — FAIL.
- QA verifies the §6.0 transient scan ran: every flagged moment in the
  source clips is either cut on or explained in the outline; an unmentioned
  flagged transient = the survey was skipped — FIX-FIRST.
- QA verifies the §1 DONOR PASS record: three shortcodes, each resolving to a
  `status: FRESH` heading in `catalog.md` (open the file and check — a
  hallucinated shortcode is a FAIL), each with its `pairs-with` line quoted
  and a beat named, each unused one rejected by one of the four §1 names.
  Missing record, or `Splice: NONE` with no named candidates = the catalog
  was not read — FIX-FIRST (thin/empty catalog per §1 is exempt). QA does not
  second-guess the rejections; it checks they exist, resolve, and name a test.
  On a channel whose file says no-splice is never a failure (customer jobs),
  the record is still required but its absence is a FIX note, not a FAIL.
- On FAIL: fix and re-QA with a fresh agent, once. Two FAILs = the
  channel's declared-stop path. A missed delivery beats a bad one.
- **A FRAME STRIP BELOW 30fps CANNOT AUDIT FRAME-LEVEL TIMING** (2026-08-18).
  A 4fps tile hid a card that changed one frame late, because it never
  sampled the boundary. Audit card gates and cut points per-frame at the
  seam, and never hardcode a text gate against a concat of variable-length
  beats — count the beats and derive the switch.
- **NEVER TRUST A FILTER OR A BUILD YOU HAVE NOT RE-INSPECTED ON THE
  DELIVERED FILE** (same run). `maskedmerge` silently did nothing and
  failed open with no error, leaving a private screen fully readable; and a
  `set -e` build aborted mid-script on a `grep` miss, leaving the PREVIOUS
  FINAL.mp4 in place where it would have staged as new. Both failures look
  like success. Re-render, re-measure, re-look — and verify the staged copy
  by SIZE AND HASH, since timestamps lie.

## 6. Splicing a banked clip into a cut (added 2026-08-21)

Written after @imabgold's first spliced reels. Before that day the account had
downloaded 43 clips and used **zero frames** across nine months: the law said
splicing was allowed and no file said HOW, so every run rebuilt with own
footage instead. §6 is that missing half.

**§6 is METHOD, not strategy.** It tells you how to execute a splice correctly.
Whether a splice is the right call is §1 and the UNPROVEN block at the end.

### 6.0 Audio-transient scan (added 2026-08-21, mandatory before any "Splice: NONE")

Before the architect declares no-splice on any channel, scan every source
clip's audio for sharp transients: 20 ms RMS windows, flag any window >=8x the
clip's median energy that also jumps >=2.5x over the previous window (claps,
knocks, slams, a laugh burst). Every flagged moment gets a 30fps frame strip
+-0.6 s and the architect looks at it by eye. The scan is
`~/Claude Code/ig-routine-core/scan-transients.sh <clips...>`; every channel
calls that one script instead of re-deriving the thresholds.

**Why:** a transient is the footage telling you where the cut goes. A clap, a
slam or a laugh is a cut-on-motion trigger, and the beat that follows it is
where a banked clip lands as the reaction/payoff (the catalog's `pairs-with`
lines are the match test). @sahilchawla.md's first ten reels, plus three more
on 2026-08-21, shipped with no splice because the 1fps visual survey walked
straight past a 0.3 s clap in drop2/02.mov at 1.37 s; Marcus found it by ear.
The clap became r4, the account's first splice.

**Rule:** a flagged transient with no cut on it is a decision the outline must
record ("transient at 02.mov 1.37 s, not cut because ..."). Silence on a
flagged transient = the survey was skipped = FIX-FIRST at QA.

### 6.1 The tests that come before any technical work (rejection names in §1)

These tests are cheap, the first two were caught by the channel owner's eye
before either reel posted, and each kills a concept outright. Run them first.

**THE LITERAL-REFERENT TEST.** The borrowed clip must be the LITERAL thing the
card names, never a mood that rhymes with it. Working examples: a machine
faceplanting IS the attempt failing; a creature refusing to get out of the
machine IS the thing not stopping; a wild celebration IS the imagined feeling.
Failing examples from the same batch: a softball fielding error is people
fumbling a ball, not a small input causing a cascade, so the sentence and the
image argued with each other; three people in costume climbing over cinema
seats is not a team, so the picture contradicted the card instead of answering
it. **If the clip is not a picture of the sentence, there is no cut here.**

**THE HOOK-LEGIBILITY TEST.** If a card's key word has a common NON-TECHNICAL
meaning, the reel is illegible cold. "Shipping" means releasing software inside
the niche and a parcel arriving outside it, and it was carrying an entire
reel's joke. §3's hook law does NOT protect against this — jargon that reads as
a different English word is not the same thing as a product specific, so the
law was followed and the reel was still unreadable. Test every hook word
against a reader who has never heard of the channel. Medical, legal and
financial channels are worse: PRP, accrual, retainer.

**⚠ THIS TEST IS GUIDANCE FOR PICKING A GOOD DONOR. IT IS NOT A PROHIBITION, AND IT MUST NOT BE
USED TO TURN A SPLICE INTO A REBUILD (Marcus, 2026-08-29).** On 2026-08-28/29 this paragraph did
exactly that twice: a spike clip was classified FORMAT-ONLY because its middle beat was a Claude
dialog box the channel could stage for free, so the architect rebuilt the concept with Marcus's own
footage instead of splicing the clip. Marcus wanted the clip spliced. In his words: *"I want you to
splice it exactly like that reel. I don't want you to take the concept. I want you to make a reel
that is almost identical to that reel. If there was a rule in the architect for this, I want that
rule to be ignored."*

**So: when the owner asks for a replica, the replica uses the DONOR'S ACTUAL FOOTAGE.** "Could we
stage this ourselves?" is a question about which donor is the strongest choice when you have a free
pick. It is never a reason to decline a splice the owner asked for, and it is never a reason to
downgrade a clip to FORMAT-ONLY on its own.

**DONOR SELECTION: splice what the channel CANNOT STAGE.** A robot driving itself
into a wall is worth borrowing because no channel here can produce one. A text
message on a phone screen is not — staging it is free, and a staged version says
something specific to this channel instead of something generic. This is a test for
picking a GOOD donor, not a reason to avoid splicing: it points customer channels
straight at the donors that matter to them (a physician cannot stage a university
lecture or a national dataset, so those are exactly what he should borrow).

**A COROLLARY THE SAME BATCH PROVED: not every sentence wants a splice.** The
literal picture of "nobody" is an empty frame, and forcing a borrowed clip in
to satisfy a requested count is what broke that reel. Own footage is the
correct answer whenever it is the literal referent. **This corollary is a
verdict on ONE candidate, never a reason to skip the §1 donor pass**: it is
cited per named clip as `OWN-IS-REFERENT` in the DONOR PASS record, after
that clip was weighed.

### 6.2 Donor survey (do this before designing around a clip)

- **There is no `ffprobe` on this machine.** Read specs from `ffmpeg -i` stderr.
- Expect **720x1280, 8-bit, bt709 SDR, 25 or 30fps** from banked clips, against
  own footage that is typically **1080x1920 HLG bt2020 10-bit with a -90 display
  matrix**. Plan for an upscale, an fps convert and a colour convert every time.
- **Find the creator's burned-in caption by measurement, not by eye.** Decode a
  sample of frames to gray and count near-white pixels per row; a burned caption
  row runs 300+ across a 720-wide frame while picture highlights stay well under.
  That returns an exact crop ceiling.
- **⚠ THE ROW-SCAN HAS FALSE NEGATIVES AND YOU MUST ASSUME IT DOES.** It missed a
  "Pubity ✓ Following" account-tag sticker (a rounded pill, not text rows) and
  small left-aligned yellow captions. **The visual check on the cropped beat is
  mandatory, never optional.**
- Reject on legibility, not on theory (`DONOR-LEGIBILITY` in the §1 record). Two donors died this way: a ball sailing
  against open sky is a dot nobody can read at reel speed, and a payoff hidden
  behind a stair rail is not a payoff.

### 6.3 Executing the splice

1. **Crop to clear the caption** with margin below the measured ceiling.
2. **Full-bleed beats letterboxing.** A blurred-fill pad read as a mistake; a
   full-bleed crop reads as borrowed footage, which is the correct register.
   Exception: genuinely wide action (a whole field) that a 9:16 crop destroys.
3. **Track a moving subject** with an expression crop (`crop=W:H:'x0+dx*n/N':y`)
   rather than a static window it walks out of.
4. **fps-convert every beat to one rate** before concat, or `-c copy` collides
   presentation timestamps at the seams and a beat plays at the wrong speed.
5. **Colour: UP-CONVERT, never tonemap.** SDR bt709 -> HLG bt2020 via
   `zscale=tin=bt709:min=bt709:pin=bt709:t=arib-std-b67:m=bt2020nc:p=bt2020:r=tv`.
   **Verify the direction by measuring**, not by trusting the flag: YAVG should
   DROP (measured 454.6 -> 326.0 at 10-bit scale, a 28% fall, matching the
   earlier `cheating_cpu` precedent of 56.8 -> 43.3).
6. **Any card sitting on a donor beat must be drawn AFTER the colour convert.**
   Drawn before, the white text is converted along with the footage and renders
   grey against the cards on own-footage beats. This shipped twice before it
   was caught; verify card peak luma is 255.
7. **Encode donor and own beats identically** (codec, pix_fmt, colour tags) so
   `-c copy` concat holds.
8. **RENDER THE BORROWED BEAT AND LOOK AT IT BEFORE LOCKING.** The first version
   of the first splice ever cut here was wrong — subject too small to read — and
   only looking caught it. This is not covered by any measurement above.
